Scoping a Social Feature with Research

Four rounds of research, before and after launch, used to scope and then check a new social engagement feature at LineLeap.

01

Context

LineLeap's product team wanted to add a new social engagement feature to the app. The idea itself wasn't the hard part. Making sure it was worth building, for the right users, in a form the engineering team could actually ship, was.

02

My role

I ran the research and built the artifacts (personas, story maps, empathy maps) that the rest of the team used to stay aligned on who the feature was for, and translated what we learned into requirements product and engineering could scope against.

03

Process

Scoping against three constraints at once

I worked with product, engineering, and design to scope the feature, balancing what users actually wanted against the business goal behind it and what was realistic to build in the timeframe. That meant cutting or simplifying parts of the original idea that didn't hold up against research or technical constraints.

Four rounds of research

I led four research rounds, two before launch and two after, to test whether the feature concept held up, and then whether it actually worked once real users had it. That combined 55 one-on-one interviews with a survey of 400+ respondents.

4 research rounds, pre- and post-launch
55 user interviews
400+ survey responses

Turning research into requirements

I built and maintained personas, story maps, and empathy maps that other teams referenced directly when scope shifted, so the team stayed aligned on who the feature was actually for even as the plan changed. Findings from each round were translated into concrete, actionable feature requirements rather than staying as research summaries no one revisited.

04

Collaboration & stakeholders

Product, engineering, and design each had a different stake in this feature. Product cared about the business goal, engineering about what was buildable on the timeline, design about the experience itself. My job was making sure the research spoke to all three, so scope decisions were made from the same evidence instead of three separate opinions.

Outcome
