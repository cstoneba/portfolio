Ask360: AI Support Assistant

Requirements, security constraints, and research for the AI assistant built into Fiserv's B2B client support portal — the tool financial-institution clients use to get help instead of opening a ticket.

Company
Fiserv
Role
Product Experience & Design Analyst
Timeline
June 2024 – present
Partners
Engineering, design, knowledge platform

01

Context

Fiserv's client support portal is how B2B financial-institution clients get help — checking on an inquiry, finding documentation, reaching a rep. Ask360 is the AI assistant layered into that portal: instead of only searching the knowledge base or filing a ticket, a client can ask a question directly and get an answer sourced from their own account and case data, with an option to escalate to a support ticket or a live rep if the assistant can't resolve it.

02

My role

I write the product requirements and user stories that translate what the assistant needs to do into something engineering, design, and the knowledge-platform team can actually build against and test. That includes the boundaries around what data the assistant is allowed to touch, not just what it should say.

03

Process

Defining what the assistant is allowed to know

Before the assistant could answer anything accurately, we had to define exactly which account and case fields it was allowed to surface — enough to give a real answer, without exposing more than a client should see through an AI interface. That constraint shaped the requirements before any conversation design happened.

Mapping the conversation

I partnered with engineering, design, and the knowledge-platform team to document 40+ conversational use cases for the portal — and, for each one, the edge cases: what happens when the assistant doesn't have enough data to answer, when a client asks something out of scope, when it needs to hand off to a live rep. I wrote the acceptance criteria for each so engineering had a clear definition of "done" and QA had something concrete to test against.

Testing it against real clients

I led B2B user research directly with financial-institution clients — one-on-one interviews plus a recurring monthly user group — to check whether the assistant's answers and escalation paths actually matched how clients asked for help in practice, not just how we assumed they would.

04

Collaboration & stakeholders

This only works if the same set of requirements makes sense to three different audiences at once: engineering needs to know exactly what's buildable and where the data boundaries are, the knowledge-platform team needs to know what content gaps to fill so the assistant has something accurate to draw from, and design needs the constraints on what the assistant can safely say before it designs the conversation itself. Keeping requirements consistent across those three groups — and re-aligning them every time research surfaced a new edge case — was most of the day-to-day work.

Outcome
