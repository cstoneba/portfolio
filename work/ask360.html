Ask360: AI Support Assistant

Redesigning how financial-institution clients get service, from the interviews that mapped the problem through an AI assistant built into the portal as one continuous journey. Piloting with 400 clients.

01

Context

Fiserv's client support portal is where financial-institution clients go when something isn't working, and getting to an answer was the problem. Ask360 is the redesign of that experience: a client describes the problem in their own words, gets documentation back for the products they actually license, and opens an inquiry from inside the same conversation only if they still need one.

02

My role

Primary designer. I ran the interviews that defined the problem, designed the experience through successive rounds of iteration, and built the working prototype with Figma Make and GitHub Copilot. I also wrote the requirements and acceptance criteria that engineering and the knowledge-platform team built against.

03

Research

I interviewed clients about how they got help today: what they searched for, where they gave up, and what they did next. Three things came back consistently.

  • Search rarely surfaced the right article.
  • What it did surface wasn't always current.
  • When search failed, the only path left was opening an inquiry and retyping the whole situation.

That reframed the problem as the whole service journey rather than a missing chatbot.

Wizard of Oz testing

I ran sessions with 10 client users, playing the assistant myself behind the prototype so people could work through real problems before any model behaviour existed. Where someone got stuck, or expected something the assistant never offered, I changed the flows and copy between sessions.

Feedback sessions

We host recurring sessions with clients to hear how they're actually using it, what works and what doesn't. Each round feeds the next set of functionality.

04

Design

One continuous journey

A chat widget dropped into the corner would have left the broken journey intact underneath it, so the assistant is native to the portal instead: describe the problem, troubleshoot with what comes back, open an inquiry from the same conversation with the context already attached. I worked in rounds, building each version into a clickable prototype so it could be judged as a real experience rather than a static screen.

An assistant that knows the client

We partnered with Sierra to build the assistant, giving it the client's details so it could help someone troubleshoot rather than return the same generic documentation. It answers against the products a client licenses and the state of their account, and it reads each user's permissions: it won't offer to open an inquiry for someone with no rights to create one, or reference an invoice they can't see.

Designing for every screen

Clients open the portal from a desk and from a phone between meetings, so the chat panel, the returned articles, and the inquiry form all had to reflow across desktop, tablet, and mobile rather than assume a full-width layout.

Writing it down so it could be built

I documented 40+ conversational use cases and their edge cases, each with acceptance criteria: what happens when the assistant doesn't have enough data, when a question is out of scope, and when documentation can't resolve it and an inquiry is the right next step.

05

Collaboration & stakeholders

Several groups had to agree on the same constraints: Sierra on what the assistant should do and with which client data, engineering on what was buildable, the knowledge-platform team on which content gaps to fill, and the owners of account and permissions data on exactly what could be exposed and to whom. Re-aligning that every time a feedback session surfaced something new was most of the day-to-day work.

06

Where it stands

400 clients in the pilot launching at the end of September
40+ conversational use cases built and documented

The pilot is the first read on whether the redesigned journey holds up outside of testing.
